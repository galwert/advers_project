\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setting}

\paragraph{Training data.}
Each batch mixes harmful prompts (from AdvBench~\citep{zou2023universal}), benign prompts (WikiText-2~\citep{wikitext}), and borderline prompts (XSTest safe subset~\citep{xstest}). Harmful prompts drive $\loss_{\text{CKA}}$, $\loss_{\text{refusal}}$, and $\loss_{\text{LM}}$; benign and borderline prompts drive $\loss_{\text{coherency}}$ and $\loss_{\text{KL}}$. Crucially, \textbf{GCG adversarial suffixes are never used during training}---the defense is attack-agnostic, relying on structural divergence rather than adversarial examples.
Without borderline prompts in training, CKA repulsion produces degenerate output on ambiguous inputs. Adding XSTest safe prompts to the benign set and protecting them with $\loss_{\text{KL}}$ and $\loss_{\text{coherency}}$ resolves this. We evaluate over-refusal on OR-Bench Hard~\citep{orbench} (zero overlap with XSTest training data).

\paragraph{Hyperparameters.}
\cref{tab:hyperparams_permodel} lists per-model settings; $\alpha\!=\!0.15$ and $\beta\!=\!1.0$ are shared across all models. Each anchor is chosen from a different model family to maximize the pre-training distribution gap (\cref{app:hyperparams:anchor}). The per-model variation in $\gamma$ reflects differences in initial CKA with the anchor: higher baseline similarity requires stronger repulsion. Larger models (9B+) use LM loss ($\delta > 0$) to prevent over-refusal. Mistral-7B requires a reduced learning rate ($5\!\times\!10^{-5}$) due to sensitivity to weight perturbation magnitude (\cref{app:hyperparams:lr}). All models converge within 200--400 steps.

\paragraph{Evaluation metrics.}
We evaluate using: \textbf{ASR} (attack success rate---self, anchor, and cross-model transfer), \textbf{FRR} (false refusal rate---refusal on \emph{unambiguously safe} prompts such as general knowledge and WikiText~\citep{wikitext} passages, detected by refusal keyword matching), \textbf{BGR} (benign garble rate---detects degenerate output missed by FRR, via a pretrained gibberish classifier with heuristic fallbacks), \textbf{MMLU}~\citep{mmlu} (5-shot accuracy), and \textbf{MT-Bench}~\citep{mtbench} (conversation quality, 1--10). For over-refusal on \emph{ambiguous} prompts, we use two separate benchmarks: \textbf{OR-Bench Hard}~\citep{orbench} (1,320 borderline-safe prompts, zero overlap with training data---our primary over-refusal metric) and \textbf{XSTest}~\citep{xstest} (250 safe-but-sensitive prompts, also used in borderline training). We note that HarmBench~\citep{harmbench} provides a complementary standardized red-teaming framework; we adopt its attack-success evaluation protocol for GCG suffix scoring. FRR and OR-Bench measure complementary failure modes: FRR detects refusal on clearly safe inputs, while OR-Bench detects over-refusal on prompts that \emph{could} be confused for harmful content.

\Cref{tab:defense_results} shows that our defense reduces cross-model ASR to \textbf{0--5\%} across all seven models, with BGR $\leq 4.4\%$ and 0\% FRR in all cases.
The defense presents a security--utility tradeoff: stronger CKA repulsion yields lower ASR at the cost of increased over-refusal or slight quality degradation (see \cref{tab:benchmark_results}).
For each model family we include multiple configurations that navigate this tradeoff differently---e.g., Mistral's \texttt{lr\_l3\_hx\_g10} achieves 3\% cross-model ASR with near-baseline MT-Bench, while \texttt{msw\_g01\_e02} trades more quality ($-0.58$ MT-Bench) for stronger overall ASR reduction.
The self-ASR reduction is a side effect: modifying representations to break cross-model similarity also disrupts the model's own pre-computed adversarial suffixes.

Run~1 (weak $\gamma=0.3$) partially reduces ASR but is insufficient. Run~2 (strong $\gamma=1.5$) eliminates transfer entirely (0\% ASR) but collapses benign representations (BGR $>$ 50\%), establishing CKA repulsion as the core mechanism. Run~3 adds refusal direction ($\alpha=0.15$) and KL-divergence ($\varepsilon=1.0$), allowing $\gamma$ to be reduced to 0.5: the refusal loss provides targeted pressure on harmful representations, while KL-divergence prevents leakage to benign prompts. Result: \textbf{0\% ASR with 0\% BGR} (benign CKA 0.867).

Early layers (0.19--0.25) achieve 0\% ASR but produce 68--80\% garbled output (MT-Bench 1.21--1.85); FRR = 0\% is misleading since the model outputs gibberish that lacks refusal keywords, demonstrating that BGR is essential for evaluating representation-engineering defenses. This is consistent with mid-layers encoding semantic representations~\citep{tenney2019bert,geva2022transformer}: CKA repulsion there disrupts shared semantic geometry without corrupting the syntactic features that earlier layers provide.

The defense navigates a tradeoff between security and over-refusal. Configurations with the lowest cross-model ASR (e.g., Yi, Llama-3, Nemo) tend to show moderate OR-Bench increases ($+1.9$ to $+29.6$\%), reflecting increased caution on borderline prompts.
